# Energy

Decode energy for Phase A, how it is measured, and everything that bounds what the number means.

This lived in a collapsed block inside the README, where it was the single longest passage in the
document and where its conclusions kept drifting out of step with the Limitations section three
screens below. The 2026-08-29 review found the two contradicting each other on whether an NVML
counter would settle the magnitude.

**The headline.** Board telemetry puts decode energy for a 400-token generation at **-37.1 %** for
MTP `n-max 2` (3980 -> 2503 J), moving toward roughly **-35 %** under two measured sensitivity
adjustments and to **-36.3 %** when the same comparison is re-read with a less-smoothed instrument, on a run that independently reproduces the uncorrected -37.1 % to 0.03 points.
That is a sensitivity-adjusted telemetry estimate. It is no longer uncrosschecked: the driver's
cumulative counter has been read against 450 records and agrees with the instantaneous power sensor,
while both disagree with the averaged field the figure is computed from. It is still not calibrated
against anything off the board.

**It is not an identical-answer comparison.** Most speculative generations follow a different token
trajectory from the baseline they are compared against, and semantic quality is not scored. Read it
as the energy of producing *an* answer of that length, not *the same* answer.

Both energy columns are decode-only. Prefill is measured separately, in its own eight-repetition
calibration per prompt, and subtracted. Counting it, the same request goes 4050 -> 2583 J, a 36.2 %
saving. Prefill is measured per arm rather than assumed constant, and it is not: 70.9 J for the
baseline against 83.2 J for `dflash2-n7`, because a speculative arm processes the prompt through
its drafter as well. `joule`, `tok/J` and `watt` appear zero times in PR #27342's 60-comment
thread.

Board telemetry puts decode energy for a 400-token answer at **-37 %** (3980 -> 2503 J). All three limits on that figure have now been measured rather than named, and two of the three bias the **relative** comparison in the same direction. `power.draw` on Ampere is a rolling average of about a second: sampled beside the instantaneous field, the two integrals agree to 0.00-0.34 % on the baselines and differ by 0.58-1.97 % on the speculative arms, always the same sign, so the averaged field understates exactly the arms being compared, worth about 1.1 points. The prefill subtraction removes a `max_tokens=1` calibration that runs on a server with the drafter already loaded, so it costs 10-17 % more energy on a speculative arm than on a baseline and takes out more than prefill: worth 0.3 to 0.8 points. The integral runs first sample to last, and the sampler's period is its nvidia-smi query plus the interval rather than the interval alone, so about 4 % of the request sits outside the window; that one limits the absolute joule totals rather than the ratio, which is why it is not one of the two above. Applying the two relative biases as a sensitivity adjustment brings the saving nearer **-35 %**. This is also not an energy comparison for an identical or quality-equivalent answer: most speculative generations follow a different token trajectory inside the window, and semantic quality is not scored. The energy columns are point estimates; no prompt-cluster interval is reported for them. `analyze.py` prints the per-arm gap and the window coverage wherever energy appears. Two facts about the instrument itself bound what any of this can mean, both from [*Part-time Power Measurements: nvidia-smi's Lack of Attention*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02741), which characterises NVIDIA's built-in sensor against external meters. The first is favourable and specific to this card: the RTX 3090 has an instant rise time, a 100 ms update period and a **100 ms averaging window**, so the sensor samples its whole runtime. The A100 and H100 average over 25 ms of each 100 ms period and therefore sample only 25 % of it -- "during the other 75 % of the time, the GPU can be using drastically different power" -- and that is where that paper's largest errors come from. **None of that applies here.** The second fact is not favourable: the sensor's steady-state error is **proportional, roughly ±5 %**, rather than the flat ±5 W NVIDIA specifies, and it runs in **both directions** depending on the individual board's component tolerances. That is larger than the 1.1-point correction above. **The claim previously made here -- that a proportional error does not cancel in a ratio -- was wrong.** Both arms run on the same board, so a common multiplicative gain `g` divides out exactly: `gE_A / gE_B = E_A / E_B`. What does not divide out is gain that varies with load, and the two arms do not draw the same power; nor does an additive offset, a nonlinearity, or a phase lag that differs with workload shape. This experiment has not separated those cases, which is the real reason the magnitude stays provisional. It is not folded into any figure here, because a bidirectional instrument error of unknown sign on this particular board cannot be corrected for, only reported. `nvmlDeviceGetTotalEnergyConsumption` has now been read rather than dismissed in advance, and it changes which instrument is in question. On `results/phase_e.json` -- 450 records, 0 incidents, the power limit stepped 420 / 250 / 150 W so that a load-dependent error can be separated from a constant one -- the counter agrees with the integral of `power.draw.instant` to within **0.15 % on every arm at every cap**, while its apparent disagreement with `energy_j` runs from -0.14 % to **+1.87 %** and regresses on the instantaneous field's own disagreement, over every file in `analysis/energy_instruments.txt` rather than this one, at **r = +0.839**. Two of the three readout paths agree and the averaged field is the one that departs, by more the harder the card works. They are paths over one sensor rather than independent instruments, so this bounds the processing and leaves the proportional bidirectional sensor error above exactly where it was: invisible to all three, and settled only by an external meter. The forum report that had been the reason to dismiss the counter, [confirmed there by a second user](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/value-from-nvmldevicegettotalenergyconsumption-seems-to-be-off-by-a-factor/336318) -- the counter about a factor of two low, the gap widening the more often power is polled -- reproduces here, and the cause is the reading itself. `analysis/nvml_polling.txt` measures it on an idle card in interleaved reps: the counter retains **99.0 %** when read twice per window, 96.2 % at 1 Hz, 54.4 % at 10 Hz and **7.4 % at 100 Hz**, while the control instrument shows the card drawing *more* at 100 Hz (36.96 W against 31.9 W), so the energy is being lost in the read rather than never drawn. Those are one run of a measurement whose watts are new each time; the ordering and the control's direction are what reproduce. `telemetry.PowerSampler` reads it exactly twice, beside the first and last power samples. The same file also bounds an assumption every energy figure here has rested on and none had tested: the harness's own 10 Hz nvidia-smi polling costs **at most about 0.5 %** -- 31.60 W with it against 31.74 W without, a difference of 1.6 standard errors, so bounded rather than resolved as zero -- against 3.8 % for reading the counter itself once a second. What the averaged field's offset is NOT, over 119 file-arm cells and 7125 records in `analysis/energy_instruments.txt` -- both base models, six named quantizations, five context lengths from 8k to 96k, three power caps, three sampler periods -- is proportional: it tracks total energy at r = +0.078, so it cannot be corrected by scaling and does not cancel between two arms drawing different power. Nor is it an integration artefact, which Phase E3 was built to test and which had been the live alternative to it being an energy difference at all. Varying nothing but the sampler's period over a 3x range of achieved rates -- 14.30, 8.43 and 4.71 Hz -- leaves the integral of `power.draw.instant` where it was, within **0.23 % of the driver's cumulative counter at every rate**, while the integral of `power.draw` sits 0.31 to 1.86 % below that counter and moves *further* from it as the grid refines. The counter is read exactly twice per window and so cannot move with the sampling rate; it is the reading that arbitrates, and it puts the averaged field in the wrong. **So the offset is a real energy difference and `power.draw` is the reading that loses it**, which is what the -36.3 % re-reading above rests on. **Phase E4 measured what the averaging loses it to.** `power.draw` is a boxcar average of `power.draw.instant`; deconvolving one against the other reads its width off directly, with no assumption about the window's ends, and the median is **1.00 to 1.10 s** on both arms at every setting, rms 1.2 to 1.6 W. Thirteen of the 75 unrolled baseline records fit at the search grid's ceiling instead, which is what a flat trace does to a deconvolution rather than evidence of a wider filter -- forcing them to 1.00 s costs 0.50 W of rms where the other 62 pay 0.11 -- and every window with a roll in it is 0 of 75. Averaging is linear and preserves the integral under it, but integrating the result ACROSS a window loses `(T/2)` times the difference between that window's two ends. With T measured the model has no free parameter and reproduces the whole unrolled offset -- **1.06** and **1.08** predicted against observed -- and **98 % and 93 %** of the offset accrues inside the first T seconds, with the middle carrying 0.06 and 3.56 J. Holding idle around the window collapses it to **0.267x** and **0.138x** while the window LENGTHENS. The arm-dependence is not a per-arm time constant, which nine files had already refused: T is one number and the speculative arm's ends differ by more. What survives at the longest roll is **5.7 J on both arms**; at the 1.5 s roll the baseline still carries 12.4 J and only `mtp-n2` has reached that floor. Its equality on the two arms distinguishes nothing, because at that roll both windows hold the same idle-to-cap excursion. **Phase E5 moved the excursion instead**, 10.8x by the power cap, and split it: a step-scaled part at **+19.7 ms** and a fixed part of **3.56 J**, intercept spread 1.05 over three independently fitted passes. The step-scaled coefficient reproduces what Phase E4 implied without being told it -- 5.7 J over a 284 W step is 20.1 ms. The **3.56 J is what remains unexplained**: an energy per window that scales with neither the step, the span, the plateau nor the total, and it is not a window mismatch between the two integrals, which cover the identical grid. The other correlations are what they were -- mean power r = +0.548, window length -0.150, SM-clock spread -0.106 -- and Phase E2 had refuted the candidate that the offset is the variation the smoothing discarded -- `sd_instant - sd` correlates with it at the wrong sign, and the joules-per-joule test that would make it a mechanism spans a factor of 574. `power_max_w` could not stand in for the spread: while the card sits at its limit, max IS the cap, so `max - mean` measures how far below the cap the mean sits rather than how much the draw moves. What is now clear is that the loss is **arm-dependent** -- across Phase E3's nine invocations it is 0.31 to 0.66 % on `baseline@pw420` against 1.41 to 1.86 % on `mtp-n2@pw420` -- which is the reason it survives a ratio; `phase_m`'s `moe-draft08b-*` arms carry a negative offset that no per-window loss produces; and across the context ladder, at a nearly constant 400 to 415 W, the offset per watt on the baselines rises from 0.0031 s at 8k to 0.0163 s at 96k. What would settle it is an external power meter, which is the reference the study above used. None of the sources in this repository's dated 2026-08-24 prior-art sweep reported an energy figure for this model and setup.
