============================================================================================

  NOT AN IDLE MEASUREMENT, and left in the table rather than dropped: model-idle at 7 s. The server had just been
  started and the card was still coming down from loading 16 GB of weights; the
  windows opened 5 s after the health check passed, which was not enough. Its mean
  power says so -- twice the idle floor -- and its offset has the other sign.

  Over the 50 windows that are idle: the offset is +0.501 W, spread 0.236, and the JOULES scale with the window while
  the WATTS do not. So it is a level difference between the two readout paths, not
  a per-window quantity -- and not a filter, because no linear filter can put
  anything at all on a window whose two ends sit at the same level.

  IT BELONGS TO THE LOWEST CLOCK STATE, AND ONLY THERE. A resident model does not
  raise the idle floor -- a server holding 16 GB and answering nothing sits at the
  same 28 W as a bare card, at 210 MHz. What a request leaves behind is a CLOCK
  state: for 15 to 20 s afterwards the card holds 1860 MHz and about 115 W, which
  is why Phase E5's windows read 128 W between requests on a 4 s roll and why that
  is not steady idle. Pinning the graphics clock reproduces that state with no
  request in the window:

    floor-210MHz     n=50    27.9 W  offset  +0.501 W
    shelf-1860MHz    n= 5    52.7 W  offset  +0.030 W

  The difference collapses by a factor of about 18 the moment the card leaves its
  lowest P-state. At the state Phase E5's roll actually sits in it is worth about
  0.03 W, or 0.2 J over that phase's 6.1 s of non-plateau window, against a fixed
  term of 1.4 to 3.6 J depending on which model is fitted. SO THIS IS NOT WHAT
  PHASE E5'S FIXED TERM IS MADE OF. `results/phase_e.json`'s 150 W arms bound it
  further, at about 0.013 W over a 44.8 s flat trace.
