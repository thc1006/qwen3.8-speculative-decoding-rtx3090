================================================================================================

  2. THE COEFFICIENT. offset = d * (p_last - p_first) on the INSTANTANEOUS field, which
     is the undelayed one. The model has no intercept, so `d origin` and `d ratio` are
     the estimates and the free fit is a DIAGNOSTIC ONLY: dp is about 380 W on every
     record because every window opens near idle and closes at full decode, and its own
     spread is the last column. Fitting an intercept against an x that does not move
     puts the relationship in the intercept and estimates the slope from the leftovers.
     On synthetic traces with a lag planted at 0.10 and at 0.25 s the free fit returned
     0.0485 and 0.0467 -- the same wrong number twice. The two estimators below return
     the planted value. Read them, not it.

  The card's sensor has a 100 ms update period. A d near it, the same across arms and
  rolls, is the model holding. A d that changes with the arm is a per-arm time constant,
  which nine files already refused once. A d that changes with the ROLL is the model
  failing outright, because a lag is a property of the instrument and not of the window.

  3. WHERE IN THE WINDOW the two integrals separate, in JOULES. The boxcar's
     weight ramps across the first T seconds and the last T, so those are the
     regions to ask about -- not a round number. T is measured in 3b below and
     used here; the head window is the first T seconds and the tail the last T.

     Joules, not the fraction of each record's own offset. That fraction is what
     this printed first and it was unreadable: the roll drives the denominator to
     near zero ON PURPOSE, so the rows for roll 1.5 and 4 came out at 491.6 % and
     -299.6 %. A ratio whose denominator the experiment is designed to abolish is

  3b. HOW WIDE IS THE AVERAGE? `power.draw` is a filtered `power.draw.instant`, so
      the width that best reproduces one from the other is a direct read of the
      filter -- with no assumption about the window's ends, which is what every
      other line here depends on. The documented figure is 'about a second' and
      nothing in this repository had measured it. A boxcar of width T behaves at a
      window edge like a delay of T/2, so this is where the coefficient in table 2
      should come from.

  A T that is the same on both arms and at every roll is a property of the driver,
  which is what it should be. One that moves with the arm is not a filter width and
  the name would be wrong.

  `at edge` counts records whose best width lands on the grid's first or last value.
  Those are not evidence of a wider filter, they are records the deconvolution
  cannot pin -- and a nearly constant trace is exactly that, because a flat signal
  carries no information about the width of a filter applied to it, so every T fits
  it about equally well and the argmin follows the noise. Checked rather than
  asserted, on the 75 unrolled baseline@pw420 records: the 13 that sit at the
  ceiling already fit no better than the rest -- best rms 1.20 W against 1.18 --
  and forcing them to 1.00 s costs a median 0.50 W more, where the 62 that do not
  hit it pay 0.11 W. The two penalties overlap, 0.66 W at worst against 0.60 W, on
  a 410 W signal. A shallow surface, not a wider filter. Read the median; the
  count is here so that a cell where MOST records hit an edge cannot be read as a
  measurement at all -- and every cell with a roll is 0 of 75.

  3c. THE WHOLE MODEL, PREDICTED PER RECORD AND COMPARED. With T measured above,
      the boxcar's loss has a closed form and no free parameter left:

          loss = (T/2) * ( mean of the last T s inside  -  the averaged field's
                           own first sample )

      the second term being, by definition of a T-wide trailing average, the mean
      of the instantaneous field over the T seconds BEFORE the window -- which is
      not sampled and does not need to be. A ratio near 1.00 at roll 0 is the model
      accounting for the offset. What survives at the longer rolls is what it does
      not account for, and that residual is the phase's second result.

  3d. WHAT THE SURVIVING RESIDUAL IS NOT. Two things the rolled rows above could
      mean, separated. The plateau is where the instantaneous field sits above 80 %
      of its own maximum, trimmed by one second at each end so the filter's ramps
      are outside it; on that stretch the two fields should agree, because a delayed
      copy of a flat signal is the same flat signal. Whatever the two fields differ
      by there is a PER-SECOND term. Everything else is at the edges.

  baseline@pw420      1.50   75       7.79    0.080       0.63    11.86     12.49
  baseline@pw420      4.00   75       7.70    0.113       0.87     5.56      6.43
  mtp-n2@pw420        0.00   75       4.16    0.249       1.05    44.98     46.03
  mtp-n2@pw420        1.50   75       4.23    0.288       1.29     4.30      5.59
  mtp-n2@pw420        4.00   75       4.08    0.146       0.57     5.78      6.35

  So the residual is NOT a per-second loss: the plateau carries under a joule of it
  on an arm whose plateau runs eight seconds and on one whose plateau runs four.
  It is at the edges, which is where the boxcar model already is -- so what survives
  is that model being slightly the wrong SHAPE, not a second mechanism beside it.

  And the two arms carrying the same residual says nothing on its own. At the longest
  roll both windows hold the same excursion, idle to the same 420 W cap and back:
  section 3's head terms are within 3 % of each other and its middle terms within
  0.5 %. Anything whose size is set by that excursion is PREDICTED to be equal on the
  two arms, so the equality distinguishes nothing. What would distinguish it is
  varying the excursion on purpose, which is Phase E5.
