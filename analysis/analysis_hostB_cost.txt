--- TEST 1: is the overhead charged to REJECTION? ---
    Model a rejection-proportional cost r (decode-steps per rejected draft token):
        k = k_verify + r * w * (1 - acceptance),  w = realised draft len / forward
        => dk/d(acceptance) = -r * w,   so  r = -slope / w
    A state-rollback account of the overhead predicts r > 0, i.e. slope < 0.
    The model holds w constant within an arm. Where the server reuses a surviving
    draft tail instead of re-drafting, w moves WITH acceptance and the slope picks
    that up rather than any rejection cost - biased positive, which reads as r < 0,
    which is this test's own conclusion. Arms whose w varies are fitted and shown,
    and excluded from the bound.

  Largest upper 95 % limit on r, over the 5 arm(s) where the model applies: +0.02131
  decode-steps per rejected draft token (mtp-n5). The bound is an upper confidence
  limit, not the largest point estimate: the maximum of several noisy points is
  biased upward and is not a bound on anything.
  At that limit the rejection term accounts for at most +2.47 % of the cycle cost (mtp-n5),
  which is the comparable figure: r is per rejected draft token, so arms at different widths
  carry the same total cost at different r.
  Acceptance spans roughly 0.096-0.918 in this data - nearly a ten-fold range -
  so a rejection-driven overhead of any consequence would have shown up here.
  Reading: the verification overhead is paid per position VERIFIED, not per
  draft REJECTED. This does not say rollback is free; it bounds how much of
  the observed cost rollback can account for.
  Not established, and not treated as overturning the reading either way: mtp-n5 (0.47 half-widths above zero)

  Two methods with independent drafters, fitted separately. What `k` measures is
  the whole speculative cycle: target verification, the drafter's own forwards,
  sampling, launch and synchronisation, output extraction and any per-step state
  management. The two methods share every one of those except the drafter.
  The two fits share 0 width(s) and cannot be compared on a common range.

  Separating the components needs per-context CUDA-event timing, a replay that
  skips drafter compute, or a profiler decomposition - none of which this file has.

--- implied optimum ---

    an interior maximum in principle. Whether the measured ladder shows one is read
    off the data below rather than asserted. An earlier version of this line stated
    that the ladder falls monotonically over every width measured. That was true of
    the dense phases it was written against and false the moment Phase M ran an MTP
    ladder that peaks at n-max 2 on both targets, and nothing would have caught it.
