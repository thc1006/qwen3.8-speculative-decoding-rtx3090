================================================================================================
DOES THE RESIDUAL FOLLOW THE STEP OR THE SPAN? THE STEP IS HELD HERE
================================================================================================
predictions from Phase E5's nine cell means, pinned in the source so this phase cannot be scored
against a moving target, and checked against `analysis/step_scaling.txt` by the test suite.

225 records over 9 invocation(s), 1 arm, 3 generation lengths, 3 rounds.

  1. THE STEP HELD AND THE SPAN MOVED, both measured per record rather than assumed.

   tokens    n   idle W   load W   step W   span s  plateau s    T s
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
      200   75    128.1    417.4    289.3     9.04       2.93  1.000
      400   75    131.4    417.7    286.3    13.89       7.79  1.050
      800   75    133.2    417.7    284.5    23.23      17.11  1.050

  the step moves 4.8 W across the three, 1.7 % of its own mean; the span moves 2.57x.
  Held to under 8 % with the span moved over 1.6x, so the two are separated here.

  2. THE RESIDUAL AGAINST WHAT E5'S TWO MODELS PREDICT FOR IT. Neither is fitted here:
     both use coefficients from Phase E5 and this phase's own measured step and span.

   tokens    n   span s  observed J  step model  span model  obs-step  obs-span
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
      200   75     9.04        7.31        9.26       12.57     -1.95     -5.26
      400   75    13.89       11.48        9.20        8.66     +2.28     +2.82
      800   75    23.23       10.85        9.16        5.73     +1.69     +5.12

  short to long: the span goes 9.0 -> 23.2 s and the residual 7.31 -> 10.85 J, a change of +3.54.
  The step model predicts no change. The span model predicts -6.84.

  3. FITTED HERE, with the step held, so a slope on 1/span is not confounded with it:

  residual on 1/span over 9 (length, round) cell means: slope -55.03 J.s, intercept +14.02 J, r -0.327
  E5's own 1/span fit, for comparison: slope +101.14, intercept +1.38 -- but there the span was moved BY the cap, which moved
  the step with it. Here it is not.

  3c. THE CONFOUND THIS PHASE INTRODUCES. Generating for longer makes the card hotter,
      so temperature moves with the length just as the span does. Between cells the two
      cannot be told apart. WITHIN a cell the span is fixed and the card still warms,
      which is where to look.

   tokens    n  temp max C   SM MHz   resid J  r(res,temp)  r(res,ordinal)  r(temp,ord)
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
      200   75        74.2     1859      7.31       +0.034          +0.041       +0.347
      400   75        80.0     1832     11.48       +0.137          +0.138       +0.794
      800   75        82.4     1802     10.85       -0.073          +0.176       +0.539

  THE LAST COLUMN IS WHY THE OTHER TWO CANNOT BE READ AS A TEMPERATURE EFFECT. The card
  warms monotonically through a pass, so inside one cell the temperature and the
  position in the pass are very nearly the same variable, and a correlation with one is
  a correlation with the other. This is the same shape of confound Phase E5 had between
  the step and the span, and naming it is all that can be done with this design.

  What the columns DO settle: a value near zero rules out a strong effect of either,
  because a real one would have to show up in a correlation with both. A large value
  settles nothing about which, and would mean section 2's between-cell change cannot be
  read as a span effect until the two are separated -- which needs the length varied
  with the thermal history held, and no phase here has done that.

  4. THE CONTRAST, PAIRED WITHIN A ROUND, which is what the models disagree about.
     Each round holds all three lengths, so differencing inside one removes the
     session drift; the spread of those differences is the error, and it is larger
     than the spread of the round means because a difference adds two variances.

  800 tokens minus 200, per round: +10.22, -4.06, +4.46
  mean +3.54 J, sd 7.19, sem 4.15 on 3 rounds (df = 2)

    against the step model, no change  predicting  +0.00 J: t = +0.85
    against the span model             predicting  -6.84 J: t = +2.50

  At this scatter, refusing an effect of 6.84 J at t = 3 would take
  about 10 rounds. This phase ran 3. A t of 2.5 on two degrees
  of freedom is a lean, not a refusal, and saying so is the result.
